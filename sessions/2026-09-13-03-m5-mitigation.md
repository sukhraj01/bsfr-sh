## Findings

- The paper's Alg. 4 has a **second** silent gap beyond DEV-04's erasure semantics and DEV-09's
  payment premise: it never says which of Case-1/2/3 applies to a given detection, and Alg. 3's
  detection is over a honeypot *sample* while Alg. 4 remediates a *system* — nothing connects the
  two. Recorded as DEV-29 rather than invented away.
- Nesting each state inside its successor (`Remediating.isolated: Isolated`, not flattened
  `infected`/`alert` fields copied forward) reads better than the flat-fields version tried first:
  the whole history is one attribute chain away from any point (`resolved.outcome_state
  .remediating.isolated.detected.infected`), and mypy enforces "no `Remediating` without a real
  `Isolated`" for free, at zero extra runtime cost.
- `caplog` was the first instinct for testing Case-3's one-log-record property, but this project's
  `util.logging.configure()` sets `propagate = False` on the `bsfr_sh` logger tree once anything
  calls it, and nothing here calls it in tests — so a handler attached directly to
  `get_logger("mitigation.cases")` is simpler and does not depend on whether some other test in the
  same process happened to configure logging first. Dead end avoided, not hit: checked
  `logging.py`'s `configure()` before writing the test, not after a flaky failure.
- No new numeric config was needed for M5: `RW_amt`/`DT-SYS_i-amt` are per-incident call arguments
  (`evaluate(ransom_amount=..., data_value=...)`), not project-wide constants like
  `transaction.payload_bytes` — there is no "the" ransom amount to declare in `configs/chain.yaml`.
